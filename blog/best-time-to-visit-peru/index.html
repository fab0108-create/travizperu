Best Time to Visit Peru: Month-by-Month Guide

Photo: Jorge Láscar, CC BY 2.0. Resized for this page.

The best time to visit Peru depends on the region. In the Andes and at Machu Picchu, the dry season runs roughly from May to September, and the rainy season roughly from December to March. The Inca Trail closes in February. April, and October to November, are the shoulder months. Lima and the Amazon do not follow that calendar.

When to visit Peru, month by month

PROMPERÚ gives the Andes two seasons. From April to October the days are sunny, the nights are cold and there is little rain. From November to March the rain is abundant. The same page says southern-hemisphere summer, 21 December to 19 March, is the rainy season in the Andes and the Amazon. The short answer above splits that calendar into a dry core, a rainy core and the shoulder months. May to September is the dry core. December to March is the rainy core. April and October still sit inside the little-rain window, so the table calls them mild. November is a shoulder month because the rains are beginning, and the table calls it rainy.

On that page, afternoons in the drier season reach up to 24 °C and nights can fall to 0 °C. In the rainy season the range it gives is 15 °C down to −3 °C. Those are ranges for the Andes, not a forecast for one morning in Cusco. This table does not add rainfall totals or station temperatures. For the figures, use the SENAMHI station normals.

PROMPERÚ describes March to May and September to November as a time of lower tourist flow, and June to August as the middle of the year, when the coast is in winter and the highlands are in their dry season. Quieter and busy in the table follow that. They are not a visitor count. In 2027 the higher daily cap includes 1 to 3 January and 29 to 31 December, so those holiday dates are high season on the Ministry calendar. A higher cap is a ceiling, not a count. The dates are in Resolución Ministerial N.° 408-2026-MC, and the visit note is Machu Picchu in 2027.

What is the best month to visit Peru?

The best time of year to visit Peru is not one month. May and September are the months to look at when you want the drier Andes without June, July and August. May opens the dry core. September is still inside the little-rain season, and it falls in the September-to-November window PROMPERÚ describes as a lower flow of visitors. June, July and August are the heart of the dry season. They sit outside those quieter windows, so they are the busier months. December to March is the time to come if green slopes matter more than a clear ridge.

When to go to Machu Picchu

Go from May to September when you want the drier path. Go from December to March when rain and cloud are acceptable. The citadel stays open through the rainy months, including February. What closed in February 2026 was the Inca Trail network, not Machu Picchu. SERNANP suspended routes 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 for the whole of February 2026 for annual maintenance, and the network reopened on 1 March 2026. SERNANP publishes each year's dates. This page does not set a February date for a later year.

The first highland nights still go to the Sacred Valley. PROMPERÚ puts Urubamba, in the valley, at 2,871 m and Cusco at 3,399 m, so the valley is the lower first stop and Cusco city comes later. That order is the altitude guide, and it does not change with the month.

Sacred Valley weather by month

Sacred Valley weather by month follows the Andes seasons on the PROMPERÚ climate page, the same months as Cusco and Machu Picchu on this page. January, February and March are rainy. April is mild: it is the start of the little-rain season, after the months the Sanctuary Master Plan describes as the heaviest rains. May, June, July, August and September are dry. October is mild, still inside the little-rain window. November is when the rains begin. December is rainy. The same when-to-travel page names the Sacred Valley, in the Cusco region, as a clear-sky place for Machu Picchu in the June-to-August dry season. The table does not claim the valley gets the same rainfall total as the citadel.

SENAMHI Cusco climate data

SENAMHI Cusco climate data stays on SENAMHI. The Cusco regional office publishes the climate outlook, and the national normals page is where the station figures are. We do not copy millimetres or degrees from those tables onto this page. A 30-year normal is not the morning you walk the valley. For the days around Machu Picchu in December and January, the station note we do cite is on that rainy-season page.

Lima and the Amazon

Lima does not share the Andes rain. The SENAMHI atlas (first edition, September 2021) puts annual precipitation in Lima and Ica at 6 mm to 10 mm, and it describes rain on the central and southern coast as drizzle, the garúa. It says the department of Lima is more humid and cloudy mainly in winter. In the table, December to March is mild with little fog, and June to September is mild and foggy. April, May, October and November sit between those seasons, so the cell says only mild. PROMPERÚ's own season dates are 21 December to 19 March for summer and 21 June to 22 September for winter, so the first days of December and June, and the last days of March and September, are the changeover. The table still uses one word for the whole month.

Do not read the coast-wide temperatures on the PROMPERÚ climate page as a Lima forecast. That page runs from Tacna to Tumbes: dense winter fog and high humidity, with a band of 12 °C to 18 °C, and a summer with little fog and a band of 25 °C to 35 °C. It also mentions strong rain from December to April, especially in higher areas. That rain is the north coast and the higher ground, not the Lima garúa.

The Amazon is not wet every week, and it is not dry. PROMPERÚ gives it a rainy season from November to March, with abundant rain, and a lower-rain season from April to October. Humidity stays very high all year. The same page says the temperature can occasionally fall to 8 °C to 12 °C between May and August, and it calls the lower-rain months a good time to visit. A different year's El Niño is a separate outlook, on our El Niño page.

What to expect in rainy season

From December to March in the Andes, expect rain, cloud and green slopes. The Sanctuary Master Plan describes the heaviest rains from January to March. Machu Picchu stays open. Rain can still change a trail or a train, which is why a private plan keeps a spare day. What to pack, how the circuits feel on wet stone, and how the holiday week differs from the rest of these months are in Machu Picchu in December and January. A rainy-season visit is ordinary Andes weather. It is not a closure.

Machu Picchu in 2027

The longer note is Machu Picchu in 2027. Resolución Ministerial N.° 408-2026-MC sets 5,600 visitors a day on 147 dates, including 19 June to 2 November 2027, and 4,500 visitors a day on every other day. That long higher-cap stretch overlaps the drier season. June, July and August, inside it, are the busier months. FAQ

What is the best time to visit Peru?

The best time to visit Peru is roughly May to September for the dry season in the Andes and at Machu Picchu, and roughly December to March for the rainy season. April and October to November are shoulder months. The Inca Trail closes in February. Lima and the Amazon follow different calendars.

What is the best time to go to Peru?

The best time to go to Peru for clearer skies in Cusco, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu is May to September. PROMPERÚ describes a lower flow of visitors in March to May and September to November, so June, July and August are the busier dry months. December to March is rainy, and Machu Picchu stays open.

When is the best time to travel to Peru?

The best time to travel to Peru depends on the region. May to September is drier in the Andes. December to March is the rainy season there. On the Lima coast, December to March has little fog and June to September is foggy. In the Amazon, November to March is rainy and April to October has fewer rains.

What is the best time to see Peru?

The best time to see Peru, for a clearer view of Machu Picchu and the Sacred Valley, is the dry season, roughly May to September. December to March is greener, and cloud is more common. Little rain in the dry months is not the same as a cloudless morning.

What is a good time to visit Peru?

A good time to visit Peru is a month you have planned for. May to September is a good time for drier weather in the Andes. December to March is a good time if you accept rain and want the green season. Machu Picchu stays open in those rainy months.

What is the best month to visit Peru?

May and September are the best months if you want the drier Andes without the middle of the year. Both sit in the little-rain season, and PROMPERÚ groups March to May and September to November with a lower flow of visitors than June, July and August.
