Your First Trip to Peru: A Calm Planning Checklist

Photo: Maurice Chédel / Wikimedia Commons, public domain. Resized for this page.

By Francisco Arriz Barrantes · TravizPeru · Updated 30 October 2026

A first trip to Peru raises the same questions every time: documents, season, altitude, Machu Picchu tickets and how many days. This checklist answers them with official sources.

1. Passport and visa

Citizens of the US, Canada, the UK and Australia do not need a tourist visa for Peru (PROMPERÚ). Your passport must be valid for at least six months from entry (Migraciones). The immigration officer sets your authorized stay on arrival. Always confirm with your own government's travel advice before flying.

2. Pick the season

Dry Andes roughly May–September; rains December–March; Inca Trail closed in February. See best time to visit Peru.

3. Plan altitude first

Lima–Cusco flight, then straight to the Sacred Valley, Cusco city later. See the altitude guide.

4. Machu Picchu tickets and trains

Tickets are personal, by circuit and hour, on the Ministry of Culture's official system; bring the same passport you used to book. See tickets and trains.

5. How many days

See how many days in Peru and a sample 10-day itinerary.

6. Health and insurance

Talk to your doctor about altitude (CDC). Choose insurance covering medical care and evacuation.

7. Safety

See Is Peru safe to visit.

FAQ

Do US citizens need a visa for Peru?

No tourist visa is needed for US, Canadian, UK or Australian citizens (PROMPERÚ).

How long must my passport be valid?

At least six months from the date you enter Peru (Migraciones).

What ID do I need at Machu Picchu?

Your ticket and your passport (Machu Picchu regulation, art. 17).

How far ahead should I book Machu Picchu?

As early as your dates are firm; time slots for high season fill up.

What is the best order for Cusco and Machu Picchu?

Sacred Valley first, then Machu Picchu, then Cusco.
