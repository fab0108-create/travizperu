How Many Days Do You Need in Peru? A Local Expert's Itinerary Guide

By Francisco Arriz Barrantes · Founder, TravizPeru · APOTUR Certified AP030-11

Right after "Is Peru safe?", this is the question I hear most: how long should the trip actually be? Too short and you'll spend it exhausted in airports and train stations. Too long and you might run out of things that feel essential. Here's how I answer it for my own clients.

The short answer

It depends on how many regions you want to see, but as a baseline:

  • 5-6 days — A fast-paced but complete introduction: Cusco, the Sacred Valley, and Machu Picchu.
  • 7-9 days — The comfortable version of the same route, with Lima added at the start or end.
  • 10-14 days — Lima, Cusco, the Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu, and Lake Titicaca, at a relaxed pace with room to breathe between destinations.
  • 15+ days — All of the above, plus the Amazon rainforest, for travelers who want the full range of what Peru offers in one trip.

There's no wrong answer here — it depends entirely on how much time you have and how much ground you want to cover. What I will say: don't try to compress the 10-14 day version into 6 days. Peru rewards a slower pace, especially around Cusco's altitude.

Why the timeline matters more than people expect: Machu Picchu access

This is the part most travelers don't realize until it's almost too late. Peru's Ministry of Culture caps daily entries to Machu Picchu — 4,500 visitors on most days, up to 5,600 on peak dates — and every single ticket must be booked in advance. There's no buying one at the gate anymore.

Booking opens on a rolling four-month window. In practice, that means:

  • June to August (peak season): Good morning entry times and add-ons like Huayna Picchu mountain routinely sell out 3-4 months ahead.
  • April and October (shoulder season): Plan on booking 1-2 months ahead.
  • November to March (low season): More flexibility, though this is also the rainy season in the Andes.

The practical takeaway: the length of your trip matters less than how far ahead you lock in your dates. This is one of the main reasons travelers come to us months before departure — we handle the booking calendar so a date doesn't quietly close while you're still deciding.

The bottom line

Start with how much time you realistically have, not with trying to see everything. A well-paced 5-day trip to Cusco and Machu Picchu will stay with you longer than a rushed 10-day trip through five cities.
