Huayna Picchu or Machu Picchu Mountain: Which Hike to Choose (2026–2027)

Photo: Pedro Szekely, CC BY-SA 2.0. Resized for this page.

By Francisco Arriz Barrantes · TravizPeru · Updated 26 November 2026 · Leer en español

Choose Huayna Picchu if you want the shorter summit and you are at ease on steep stone stairs. Choose Machu Picchu Mountain if you want the longer, high-effort walk and the view toward Salkantay and Pumasillo. Choose neither if someone is uneasy on a narrow mountain path, or if the hike would be the first hard day at altitude. We request the tickets as soon as you confirm, subject to availability.

The two hikes, side by side

They are two different tickets for the Machu Picchu visit, not a second pass you buy at the top. Since 1 June 2024 the official list, under Resolución Ministerial N.° 528-2023-MC, has been three circuits and ten routes. Huayna Picchu is the peak in the classic photograph. Its official name is Montaña Waynapicchu: Route 3-A, Circuit 3, Machupicchu Realeza. Machu Picchu Mountain is the other mountain ticket: Route 1-A, Circuit 1, Panorámico. Route 3-B shares Circuit 3 and does not go to the summit. How the codes get mixed up is in the 2026 ticket guide.

The citadel sits at 2,430 m in the UNESCO listing of the sanctuary. Tu Boleto's record for the Llaqta uses the same 2,430 m. The day still includes the citadel, on the route printed on the ticket.

Huayna Picchu, Route 3-A

Tu Boleto describes a round trip of about 2.4 km and rates the effort high. The walk starts in the agricultural sector, where the classic photograph is taken close to the urban zone, then goes by the Temple of the Sun, the House of the Inka, the ceremonial fountains and Tres Portadas. The path then climbs Waynapicchu, with views of the city, and returns by the Temple of the Condor. Circuit 3's own introduction says the temple walk needs little effort, and that Huayna Picchu, Huchuy Picchu and the Great Cavern need more effort and more time.

PromPerú calls the climb not easy: hundreds of steps that begin at the Sacred Rock, and a view of Machu Picchu from above. The Machu Picchu page on the same site gives the mountain a height of 2,720 m, describes a steep incline, a trail that narrows, stairways carved into the rock, and terraces that appear to hang over the drop. The separate Waynapicchu page prints 2,445 m in its general-information box. The two PromPerú pages do not match, so this article cites both and does not invent a third survey.

In June 2026 the Ministry closed Route 3-A from 1 to 30 June for conservation: prehispanic stairways, retaining walls, drainage, and steel-cable railings on the steeper, higher-risk sections, from the control point to the summit. It reopened on 1 July 2026. That closure is over. A later closure would be a new notice on gob.pe. We do not plan as if June is shut in every year.

Machu Picchu Mountain, Route 1-A

Tu Boleto describes a round trip of about 3.8 km and rates the effort high. From the summit it names the snow peaks Salkantay and Pumasillo, and panoramic photographs of the landscape around the Inka city. Circuit 1 is the panoramic circuit. Its text says the four routes lead along narrow paths to the high part of Machu Picchu, and that each route asks for a different effort. Route 1-A is the high-effort one of that set.

The resolutions and the Tu Boleto route text we checked do not print a summit altitude for Machu Picchu Mountain. This page does not supply one. The citadel figure above, 2,430 m, is the sanctuary, not this summit.

The classic photograph does not require this mountain, or Huayna Picchu. The Ministry's note of 31 May 2024 says every circuit allows that photograph. Tu Boleto also places it on routes that stay in the citadel, including 1-B, 2-A and 3-B.

Who each hike suits

Fear of heights

Huayna Picchu is the one to decline if steep stairs are the problem. PromPerú describes a narrowing trail, stairs cut into the rock, and terraces over the drop, and the 2026 works renewed cable railings on the steepest sections. Machu Picchu Mountain is still a high-effort mountain path. Circuit 1's own text says those routes use narrow paths, so it is the wrong choice for someone who wants to stay off a narrow trail. The citadel routes are the fit in that case. Route 3-B is the designed walk of the same Royalty circuit, without the summit, and Tu Boleto says one stretch of it allows a wheelchair.

Fitness

Tu Boleto marks both mountain routes as high effort. Huayna Picchu is the shorter distance and the steeper character. Machu Picchu Mountain is the longer distance, about 3.8 km against about 2.4 km. From 1 January 2027, Resolución Ministerial N.° 407-2026-MC says these alternate routes are voluntary and that the visitor should be in good physical condition. The same resolution gives an average of three hours between entry and exit at the Waynapicchu control, and four hours at the Machu Picchu Mountain control.

Seniors and families

The calm private visit, for most seniors and for most families, is a citadel route and an easy first highland day. That pacing is in Machu Picchu for seniors and, in Spanish, Machu Picchu para adultos mayores. A mountain belongs only to the people who want a high-effort hike and are comfortable with the path. Tickets are per person and per route. On a private day, hikers can hold a mountain route and the rest of the party a citadel route, if both are available. They will not share the same path. This is general travel information, not medical advice.

Photography

PromPerú describes Waynapicchu's silhouette in the background of the photographs taken at Machu Picchu, and the view from the summit looking down on the citadel. Choose it when that is the picture you came for, and the stairs are acceptable. Choose Machu Picchu Mountain when the picture is the wide one: Tu Boleto names Salkantay and Pumasillo from that summit. Choose a citadel route when the classic photograph is enough. You do not need a summit for it.

How it fits a private itinerary

Both hikes are a poor first day after the flight into Cusco. The order we start from puts the first highland nights in the Sacred Valley, which is lower than the city, and leaves Cusco for later. Altitudes and the cases where a first night in the city still fits are in Cusco or the Sacred Valley first. The fuller note, including what to ask a doctor, is how we prepare for altitude. The mountain day comes after those easier nights, not on the afternoon you land.

The early hours make the night before a practical choice. In 2026 the annex puts Huayna Picchu at 7:00 and 10:00, and Machu Picchu Mountain at 7:00 and 9:00. For a 7:00 entry, the calm plan is a night in Machupicchu Pueblo, the town also called Aguas Calientes, and the bus from town up to the gate in the morning. What changes on 1 January 2027

Resolución Ministerial N.° 407-2026-MC rewrites the alternate-route rules from 1 January 2027. Visits dated through 31 December 2026 keep the previous text. From 2027, entry to Machu Picchu Mountain and to Waynapicchu is until 10:00. Visitors leave either summit by 12:00. Machu Picchu Mountain is back by 14:00. The average time from the mountain control is four hours on Route 1-A and three hours on Route 3-A. Each route then allows a further two and a half hours in the Llaqta, counted from entry. The maximum for the mountain plus the citadel is six and a half hours on Machu Picchu Mountain and five and a half hours on Huayna Picchu. The visitor registers on the way in and on the way out, and should be in good physical condition.

The annex to Resolución Ministerial N.° 408-2026-MC still lists 400 places a day for Waynapicchu and 350 for Machu Picchu Mountain, in the regular season and in the high season. They sit on the 6:00 and 8:00 entry rows: 200 and 175 at each of those hours, with the same online and in-person split as in 2026 (175 plus 25, and 150 plus 25). The footnote sets registration at the mountain control from 7:00 to 8:00 and from 9:00 to 10:00, for both mountains. Daily caps for the whole citadel, and the fact that no sale date for 2027 was published when we checked, are in Machu Picchu in 2027.

FAQ

Huayna Picchu or Machu Picchu Mountain: which should we choose?

Choose Huayna Picchu if you want the shorter summit and you are at ease on steep stone stairs. Choose Machu Picchu Mountain if you want the longer, high-effort walk and the view toward Salkantay and Pumasillo. Choose neither if someone is uneasy on a narrow mountain path, or if the hike would be the first hard day at altitude.

Which official route is each mountain?

Huayna Picchu is Montaña Waynapicchu, Route 3-A, in Circuit 3, Machupicchu Realeza. Machu Picchu Mountain is Route 1-A, in Circuit 1, Panorámico. Route 3-B is the designed walk of Circuit 3 and does not go to the summit. That list has been in force since 1 June 2024.

How many places are there in 2026, and at what hours?

The 2026 capacity annex lists 400 places a day for Huayna Picchu, at 7:00 and at 10:00, 200 at each hour (175 online and 25 in person). Machu Picchu Mountain has 350 places a day, at 7:00 and at 9:00, 175 at each hour (150 online and 25 in person). The same split is in the regular season and in the high season. We do not guarantee a place.

What changes on 1 January 2027?

Resolución Ministerial N.° 407-2026-MC applies to mountain visits from that date. Entry to both mountains is until 10:00, and visitors leave the summit by 12:00. Machu Picchu Mountain is back by 14:00. The 2027 capacity annex puts the places on the 6:00 and 8:00 entry rows, and its footnote sets mountain-control registration at 7:00 to 8:00 and 9:00 to 10:00. Visits dated through 31 December 2026 keep the earlier rules.

Can seniors, families, or someone afraid of heights do these hikes?

Neither mountain is the calm default. PromPerú describes Waynapicchu as a steep climb of hundreds of steps, with a narrowing trail. Tu Boleto rates both routes high effort, and Circuit 1's own text says its routes use narrow paths. A person who is uneasy with steep stairs should stay off Huayna Picchu. A person who is uneasy on a narrow mountain path should stay on a citadel route. Seniors and families usually do better on that citadel route. This is general information, not medical advice.
