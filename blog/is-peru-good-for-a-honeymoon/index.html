Is Peru good for a honeymoon?

Photo: Jorge Láscar, CC BY 2.0. Ollantaytambo. Resized for this page.

By Francisco Arriz Barrantes · TravizPeru · Updated 24 November 2026 · Leer en español

Yes. Peru is a good honeymoon when the two of you have the trip to yourselves, the days are long enough to stay unhurried, and the Andes are reached gradually. On PROMPERÚ's calendar the drier season in the Andes is April to October. As a suggestion, a focused private stay is about 8 to 12 days. That range is not a package, and it is not a minimum the authorities set.

When to go

PROMPERÚ gives the Andes two seasons. From April to October the days are sunny, the nights are cold and there is little rain. It calls that the better time to visit, with afternoons up to 24 °C and nights that can fall to 0 °C. From November to March the rain is abundant, between 15 °C and −3 °C on the range it publishes. The same page says southern-hemisphere summer, 21 December to 19 March, is the rainy season in the Andes. A honeymoon does not have to chase the driest month. It does have to know which season it is in.

The drier months are also when Machu Picchu is busier, because that is when more people want the clear view. The rainy months are greener, and the citadel is open. Neither season is closed to a couple. The month-by-month picture, coast and Amazon included, is the best time to visit Peru. December and January at the citadel have their own note: Machu Picchu in the rainy season.

How many days, as a suggestion

These ranges are for planning. They are not a product.

  • About 8 to 12 days for Lima, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu, with the first highland nights lower than Cusco. That is the shape of a honeymoon that still has empty hours in the day.
  • Below that, drop a place. A shorter trip that tries to add the lake, the Amazon and a second ruin is how the days stop feeling like a honeymoon. The Peru-only lengths are in how many days in Peru.
  • Lake Titicaca only with extra days. The lake page plans it after nights in the valley or in Cusco, because the journey crosses a pass higher than Cusco. The honeymoon page adds it only when you have the days and you want it.

Private, or a group

A honeymoon with us is private: your party of two, your guide, your hour. That is the trip on the honeymoon page, and the wider private shape of the country is luxury private tours. A group tour uses one speed and one route. Both can see the same places. The difference is whose clock the day runs on, which matters more at altitude than it does in Lima. The plain comparison is private or group. We do not sell a shared departure and call it a honeymoon.

Altitude, paced for two

The CDC Yellow Book says acclimatizing for a minimum of 2 to 3 nights at around 2,450 to 2,750 m, before going higher, is markedly protective against acute mountain sickness. It also says not to go from low altitude to a sleeping altitude above 2,750 m in one day, to avoid alcohol for the first 48 hours, and to keep the first 48 hours to mild exercise. This is general travel information. It is not medical advice, and it is not a promise about how either of you will feel.

PROMPERÚ puts Cusco at 3,399 m and Urubamba, in the Sacred Valley, at 2,871 m. UNESCO puts Machu Picchu at 2,430 m. Lima, where the trip starts, is on the coast. Urubamba is lower than Cusco and it is still above 2,750 m, so it is not the intermediate band the CDC names. It is the lower first stop this map allows, which is why the highland nights begin in the Sacred Valley and Cusco city comes later, usually after Machu Picchu. Two or three of those valley nights is the practical reading of the CDC's 2 to 3 nights. The longer note is how we prepare for altitude.

FAQ

Is Peru good for a honeymoon?

Yes, when the trip is private, the days are enough to stay unhurried, and the Andes are reached gradually. PROMPERÚ's drier months in the Andes are April to October. The citadel is also open in the rainy season, from November to March.

When should a couple go?

There is no single best month. April to October is the drier season in the Andes, with sunny days and cold nights, and it is also the busier season at Machu Picchu. November to March is rainy. Both can work for a honeymoon. The choice is weather against how full the routes are.

How many days does a Peru honeymoon need?

As a suggestion, not a package, about 8 to 12 days covers Lima, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu, with nights arranged so the altitude is gradual. Fewer days means dropping a place, not rushing Cusco. Lake Titicaca needs more days, and it is a poor first night.

Is a private trip better than a group for a honeymoon?

A private trip is your party and your pace, which is how we design a honeymoon. A group tour uses one speed and one route. Both can see Peru. The difference is whose clock the day runs on.

How should we pace the altitude?

The CDC recommends at least 2 to 3 nights at intermediate altitude, around 2,450 to 2,750 m, before going higher, and advises against a first-night sleep above 2,750 m when you come up from low altitude. Cusco is 3,399 m. We start in the Sacred Valley, which is lower than Cusco and still high, and leave Cusco city for later. This is not medical advice.
