Machu Picchu in December and January: Is the Rainy Season Worth It?

Photo: Rom, CC BY-SA 2.0. Resized for this page.

By Francisco Arriz Barrantes · TravizPeru · Updated 23 November 2026 · Leer en español

Yes, Machu Picchu is open in December and January. It is rainy season, so cloud and showers are part of the trip, and rain can sometimes change the train. It is worth it when you pack for wet stone and keep a flexible day in the plan.

What the weather is actually like

Around Machu Picchu the rainy season runs roughly from November to March. The Plan Maestro del Santuario Histórico de Machupicchu describes the heaviest rains from January to March. The plan's climate note for the protected area describes a dry season and three months of much heavier rain, January to March, especially higher up.

SENAMHI 1991-2020 normals at the Machu Picchu station put December at about 275 mm of rain and January at about 341 mm. Daily highs sit around 21-22 °C and lows around 12 °C. Those figures are long-term averages, not a promise about the morning you walk in. A month can still hold bright hours, and a "dry" forecast can still bring a shower over the ridge.

For the wider country, including Lima's different calendar, see the best time to visit Peru. A rainy-season trip is ordinary Andes weather. El Niño is a separate outlook: what the official centers are saying is on our El Niño page.

The citadel stays open

The Llaqta of Machu Picchu stays open through December and January. It also stays open in February. What closes every February is the Inca Trail network, the Red de Caminos Inka, for maintenance. SERNANP announces that closure each year. In 2026 the network reopened on 1 March. This page does not set dates for later years. If your dates reach into February, plan the citadel, not the classic Trail, until SERNANP publishes that year's notice.

December and January themselves sit outside that February closure. Rain can still make paths slick. The same altitude rules apply in the wet months as in the dry ones: see how we prepare for altitude. Wet stone is harder underfoot, which matters on a slower visit; the pacing notes are in Machu Picchu for seniors.

Circuits, routes and tickets

Since 1 June 2024 the visit uses 3 circuits and 10 routes (Resolución Ministerial N.° 528-2023-MC; the list is on machupicchu.gob.pe). Each ticket is one route and one entry time, sold on the official platform tuboleto.cultura.pe.

  • Circuit 1, Panoramic: Machupicchu Mountain and the Upper Terrace. Intipunku and the Inca Bridge are offered only in high season.
  • Circuit 2, Classic: the designed route and the Lower Terrace. This is the steadier walk when the stone is wet. The mountain routes ask for more grip and more stairs.
  • Circuit 3, Royalty: Waynapicchu Mountain and the designed route. The Great Cavern and Huchuypicchu are offered only in high season.

Resolución Ministerial N.° 285-2025-MC sets the 2026 capacity. High-season dates, including 1 January 2026 and 30 and 31 December 2026, allow up to 5,600 visitors a day. Other days allow up to 4,500. The Ministry of Culture sets the capacity and the season dates each year, so a later calendar is not the 2026 list repeated. The route-by-route picture is in our 2026 tickets guide.

Christmas and New Year week can be busy even when the landscape is green. Outside that holiday week, December and January are often quieter than June, July and August. We request your tickets as soon as you confirm, subject to availability. A sold-out hour on one route is not the same as a closed citadel.

Trains, rain and a spare day

The train from the Sacred Valley to Machu Picchu Pueblo (Aguas Calientes) is still the way in. How to choose the service is the train guide. What the line is doing on a given morning is the Machu Picchu train status page, which we date from Lima. Rain, and sometimes a landslide, can delay or suspend trains. That is why a private December or January itinerary keeps a spare day in Cusco or the Sacred Valley, so one disrupted morning does not erase the visit.

An authorized bus runs from the town up to the gate. We do not ask guests to walk the road in the rain. If the morning is closed in, we use the flexible day rather than promising a clear view at a fixed hour. Cloud on the saddle often lifts later in the morning. It does not always do so.

Why it can still be the right trip

The citadel in cloud is the photograph at the top of this page: terraces, wet stone, and the peak only partly there. Many travelers come in these months for that green season, and for a citadel that is calmer once the holiday week has passed. A private honeymoon can use the same logic: your own guide, a Classic or Royalty route chosen for the weather, and room to move the hour if the ridge is shut in. It is a poor match if you need a guaranteed blue sky or a classic Inca Trail hike in February.

On a private visit we set the circuit with you before we request the ticket, start from the Sacred Valley so the altitude is gradual, and keep the spare day unscheduled on purpose. The shape of that day is the private Machu Picchu tour.

FAQ

Is Machu Picchu open in December and January?

Yes. The citadel stays open in December and January. It also stays open in February, when the Inca Trail network closes for maintenance.

Is December and January the rainy season?

Yes. The rainy season around Machu Picchu runs roughly from November to March. The Sanctuary Master Plan describes the heaviest rains from January to March.

How much rain falls, and how warm is it?

SENAMHI 1991-2020 normals at the Machu Picchu station show about 275 mm of rain in December and about 341 mm in January. Daily highs are about 21-22 °C and lows about 12 °C. These are long-term averages, not a forecast for one day.

Is the Inca Trail open in December and January?

December and January sit outside the yearly February closure. The Inca Trail (Red de Caminos Inka) closes every February for maintenance, and SERNANP announces the dates each year. This page does not set dates for later years.

Are Machu Picchu tickets easier in these months?

Not always. Christmas and New Year week can be busy. For 2026, high-season dates, including 1 January and 30 and 31 December, allow up to 5,600 visitors a day; other days allow up to 4,500. We request your tickets as soon as you confirm, subject to availability. The Ministry of Culture sets capacity and season dates each year.

What should I pack, and how do I plan for rain?

Bring a rain jacket or poncho and shoes that can get wet. Keep a spare day in Cusco or the Sacred Valley in case rain or a landslide changes the train. Morning cloud often lifts, and outside the holiday week the mountains are greener and quieter.
