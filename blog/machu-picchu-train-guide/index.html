Machu Picchu Train: How to Get There by Train (Guide)

Yes. The train is the main way to reach Machu Picchu town, also called Aguas Calientes. PeruRail and Inca Rail both run it. Board at Ollantaytambo in the Sacred Valley. PeruRail also boards at Cusco-area Poroy and San Pedro from May through December. From town, the Consettur bus climbs to the citadel. Seats are subject to availability.

Is there a train to Machu Picchu?

Yes. The Machu Picchu railway ends in the town, not at the gate. PeruRail calls the train the most convenient way to reach Machu Picchu, and it is specific about the stop: Machu Picchu Pueblo, the town also called Aguas Calientes. From there you take a bus up to the citadel. Inca Rail says the same thing in its own words: every train arrives in the town, and the citadel is a bus after that. Travelers also ask for the tren, or the trem, to Machu Picchu. It is this ride.

Two companies carry passengers, PeruRail and Inca Rail. I do not keep a favorite logo. I keep the departure that can still meet the hour on a citadel ticket. That ticket is a different purchase. PeruRail's destination page says you buy it on the Ministry of Culture's site, tuboleto.cultura.pe, for a date, a circuit, and an entry time, and that the ticket is personal. I wrote the 2026 buying notes in the ticket guide. I request the train seat and the citadel ticket subject to availability. I do not promise either one.

PeruRail's train services page puts the ride at about 1.5 to 2 hours from Ollantaytambo and about 3.5 to 4 hours from Cusco or Poroy. Its schedules page says those timetables can change. The morning itself is the dated note I keep from Lima, Machu Picchu train status. This page is how to choose the train when the line is running. If the two disagree, believe the status page.

Machu Picchu train stations

The station name on the ticket matters more than the city you slept in. Read it before you tell anyone where to be at dawn.

Ollantaytambo

This is the station I use for most private trips. It is in the Sacred Valley, at Av. Ferrocarril s/n. Both companies board here.

PeruRail's Expedition route page says the Ollantaytambo service runs all year, about 1 hour 30 minutes toward Machu Picchu Pueblo and about 2 hours on the way back. Inca Rail's FAQ says the train takes about 1 hour 40 minutes and covers 43.4 kilometers, and that the Ollantaytambo station is open all year. PeruRail's train services page also calls Ollantaytambo the station with the widest range of timetables. Widest is not the same as a free seat. The seat is subject to availability.

Cusco: Poroy, San Pedro, and Wanchaq

The Cusco-area station is a PeruRail question, and it changes with the month. Inca Rail does not list Poroy or San Pedro. Its trains, its FAQ says, depart from two stations: Ollantaytambo and Machu Picchu. A Cusco start with Inca Rail is a bus, not a platform in the city.

On PeruRail's Expedition route page, checked on 25 November 2026:

  • San Pedro, Calle Cascaparo s/n, Cusco. About 4 hours to Machu Picchu Pueblo. Listed from May through December. The return text says the station is about a 5-minute walk from the main square.
  • Poroy, Calle Roldan s/n, Poroy district. About 3 hours out, and about 3 hours 30 minutes back. Listed from May through December. The return text says Poroy is about a 30-minute drive from the main square.
  • Wanchaq, Av. Pachacuteq s/n. A bus and then a train, about 4 hours 40 minutes. Listed from January through April on that page.

PeruRail's bimodal page says the bus-plus-train runs all year on some frequencies, and that from January through April it applies to every frequency that starts or ends in the city of Cusco. The bus leaves Wanchaq, about five minutes from the center, and passengers board the train at Ollantaytambo or Pachar. Pachar is a transfer point on selected schedules. It is not the Ollantaytambo station.

The timetable page splits the same way. The January to April board I read shows Wanchaq and Ollantaytambo as the Cusco-area and valley origins, not Poroy or San Pedro. The May to December board shows San Pedro and Poroy, and it still shows some Wanchaq departures. A traveler in June should not assume every Cusco service has moved to Poroy. Read the station name on the ticket.

PeruRail's station list also names Urubamba, Av. Ferrocarril s/n, for the Vistadome. I do not use it as the default. Confirm it on the timetable for your date.

Inca Rail's Cusco bus leaves from Av. El Sol 843. The FAQ says to be there 30 minutes before the bus, that the ride to Ollantaytambo is about 2 hours, and that staff then walk you about 100 meters to the train. The train after that is about 1 hour 40 minutes. PeruRail's how-to page, measuring from the Plaza de Armas, lists the Ollantaytambo station at about 1 hour 30 minutes by road. Use the time printed on the ticket you hold. Inca Rail also says its departure stations may vary by season, while Ollantaytambo and Aguas Calientes stay open all year. If the ticket names a station I have not described, believe the ticket.

Hiram Bingham is the one I will not smooth over. PeruRail's FAQ says the train-only service, from April through December, boards at Poroy or Ollantaytambo, and that from January through April it is a private bus from Poroy to Belmond Hotel Rio Sagrado and then the train. That FAQ says the ride from Cusco center out to Poroy is not included. The bimodal page describes a different bus start: Wanchaq to Hotel Rio Sagrado, then the train. The 2026 timetable PDFs still hosted on PeruRail, May to December and January to April, say the train runs every day except the last Sunday of the month. The May to December sheet shows Poroy. The January to April sheet shows a bus from Wanchaq. I am not going to pick a winner among pages that disagree. Confirm the station and the day on PeruRail before you promise anyone a platform.

Machu Picchu station in Aguas Calientes

The Machu Picchu train station is in the town. PeruRail's address is Av. Machu Picchu Pueblo s/n, Barrio Las Orquídeas, Machu Picchu Pueblo. Inca Rail's address is Train Station S/N, Km 111, in Aguas Calientes.

PeruRail's how-to page tells you to board the Consettur bus, which it calls the authorized company, from Machu Picchu Pueblo to the citadel entrance, and it gives that ride as 30 minutes. Inca Rail's FAQ says the bus from town to the citadel is about 30 minutes, and that the walk up is about 2 and a half hours. I do not plan a timed entry around the walk unless you already know you want it. The visit itself is the Machu Picchu page.

PeruRail's FAQ says Expedition and Vistadome tickets do not include the citadel entrance or the bus up and back. Count the bus as its own step. Hiram Bingham is the exception in that FAQ, and Inca Rail says a First Class ticket includes the citadel bus. An entrance an operator lists inside a package is still subject to availability. It is not a promise from me.

These are small trains. PeruRail's train services page allows one backpack up to 8 kg or one carry-on up to 12 kg on Expedition, Vistadome, and Vistadome Observatory. The Expedition route page states 8 kg and 115 cm, height plus length plus width. Inca Rail's FAQ allows one piece of hand luggage, no more than 8 kg and 118 linear centimeters, and says larger bags can be left at Av. El Sol 843 or at the Ollantaytambo ticket office. Read the allowance on the booking before you pack the suitcase. Leave the large bag at the hotel.

PeruRail vs Inca Rail

Same gorge, same town, different cars. The PeruRail train to Machu Picchu is the one with Poroy, San Pedro, Wanchaq, Urubamba, and Ollantaytambo on its station list, and the one that sells Hiram Bingham, a Belmond train. PeruRail's destination guide highlights three services for the route: Expedition, Vistadome, and Vistadome Observatory. The FAQ still documents Hiram Bingham beside them.

Inca Rail boards at Ollantaytambo. Its comparison page, which I read the same day, lists five classes: The Voyager, The 360°, The Prime, The First Class, and The Private. That table says a round-trip transfer between Cusco and Ollantaytambo is included on each of them. The FAQ is where the bus is described, from Av. El Sol 843. The same FAQ, answering what a ticket includes, names four services and uses one name the comparison page does not: The Voyager, The 360, The Premium & Lounge, and The First Class. I am not going to invent what The Premium & Lounge includes. If that name is the one on your booking screen, read that screen.

I choose the hour first and the carriage second. If the car you wanted is full and the other company still has the departure that meets the ticket, I take the hour. Seats are subject to availability. This page does not print a fare. Buy on the company site, PeruRail or Inca Rail, the day you book.

Which class for a luxury or private trip

On a private trip the class is a question of what the train day is for.

If the day is the citadel, I request Expedition or The Voyager. The seat and the hour are the point. If the ride is why you came, and the arrival still protects the gate, I look at Vistadome, Vistadome Observatory, or The 360°. If the trip is a celebration, the operators' own pages point to Hiram Bingham, The First Class, or The Private. The Prime sits between a scenic car and a private one.

I still match the arrival to the entry hour. A beautiful carriage that reaches town after the gate has opened is the wrong train. For an early gate, sleep in Machu Picchu Pueblo the night before. A same-morning train from Ollantaytambo is a poor tool for a dawn entry. I request the seat subject to availability. I do not guarantee it, and I do not guarantee the citadel ticket, including when an operator lists an entrance inside a package.

The private day is the private Machu Picchu tour. A longer route, with the valley and the cities held to the same standard, is luxury private tours in Peru.

The train and the Sacred Valley

Ollantaytambo is how I join the railway to the valley. You sleep down there, you have breakfast in town, and you walk to a station PeruRail runs all year and Inca Rail keeps open all year. You are not crossing Cusco at dawn for a bus that then becomes a train.

That Cusco bus-plus-train is the right tool when the trip truly starts in the city and the entry hour is late enough to absorb the extra time. On the private trips I design, it is the exception. The valley day before the platform is the Sacred Valley page.

Buy the return with the outbound. A one-way seat is how an afternoon ends in Aguas Calientes in front of a board that has nothing left. The name on the train ticket should match the passport you will show at the station. If you are two, both names go in before anyone pays.

The train in the rainy season

PeruRail's how-to page calls November through March the rainy season, with days around 19 to 21°C. The railway is not parked for those months. The schedules page publishes a January to April timetable, and the Expedition page keeps Ollantaytambo all year. What changes, on that Expedition page, is the Cusco end: from January through April the listed Cusco route is the bus from Wanchaq.

A printed timetable is not a promise that your morning will run. I wrote the December and January version of that problem in the rainy-season guide. When the question is the line itself, the note I date from Lima is the train-status page. For a trip that is still a year out, the planning page is Machu Picchu in 2027. The timetable for that year will belong to the operator, not to this article.

On a private December or January itinerary I keep a spare day in Cusco or the Sacred Valley, so one disrupted morning does not erase the visit. Whenever the seat is, it is subject to availability.

Questions travelers ask

Is there a train to Machu Picchu?

Yes. PeruRail and Inca Rail both run trains to Machu Picchu town, also called Aguas Calientes. The train stops in the town and does not go up to the citadel. PeruRail names the Consettur bus for that ride and gives it as about 30 minutes. Inca Rail also says the bus takes about 30 minutes. Seats are subject to availability.

How do you get to Machu Picchu by train?

Board at Ollantaytambo, in the Sacred Valley. PeruRail and Inca Rail both use that station, and both say it runs all year. From May through December, PeruRail also lists Poroy and San Pedro in the Cusco area. From January through April, PeruRail's Expedition page lists a bus from Wanchaq and then the train. Inca Rail's Cusco option is its own bus from Av. El Sol 843 to Ollantaytambo, then the train. Seats are subject to availability.

Where is the Machu Picchu train station?

In Machu Picchu Pueblo, the town also called Aguas Calientes, not at the citadel. PeruRail's address is Av. Machu Picchu Pueblo s/n, Barrio Las Orquídeas. Inca Rail's address is Train Station S/N, Km 111.

Where does the Cusco to Machu Picchu train leave from?

It depends on the month and the company. From May through December, PeruRail's Expedition page lists San Pedro, at Calle Cascaparo s/n, and Poroy, at Calle Roldan s/n. From January through April, that page lists a bus-plus-train from Wanchaq, at Av. Pachacuteq s/n. The May to December timetable still shows some Wanchaq departures too. Inca Rail does not list Poroy or San Pedro. Its Cusco start is a bus from Av. El Sol 843. Confirm the station on your ticket.

What is the PeruRail train to Machu Picchu?

PeruRail runs the Expedition, the Vistadome, the Vistadome Observatory, and Hiram Bingham, a Belmond train, to Machu Picchu Pueblo. Inca Rail is the other passenger company on the line. Its comparison page lists The Voyager, The 360°, The Prime, The First Class, and The Private. This page does not print a fare. Seats are subject to availability.

Does the Machu Picchu train run in the rainy season?

PeruRail calls November through March the rainy season, and it still publishes a January to April timetable. Ollantaytambo is listed all year. A printed departure is not a promise that your morning will run. Read the December and January guide and the train-status page. The seat is subject to availability.
