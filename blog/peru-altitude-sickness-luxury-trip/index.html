Peru Altitude Sickness: How to Prepare for a Comfortable Trip

Photo: Jorge Láscar from Australia / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0. Resized for this page.

By Francisco Arriz Barrantes · TravizPeru · Updated 30 October 2026

Altitude is the one factor that shapes every good Peru itinerary. The answer is not to avoid the Andes but to reach them gradually. This is general travel information based on the CDC; it is not medical advice.

How high is it?

Cusco 3,399 m (PROMPERÚ) · Urubamba, Sacred Valley 2,871 m · Machu Picchu 2,430 m (UNESCO) · Lima at sea level. Lake Titicaca (Puno) is higher than Cusco.

What the CDC says

  • Altitude illness can affect any traveler sleeping above about 2,450–2,750 m; fitness does not protect you.
  • Ascend gradually and avoid going from low altitude to a sleeping altitude above 2,750 m in one day.
  • For destinations like Cusco, the CDC notes travelers can first sleep lower for 1–2 nights and then ascend.
  • No alcohol and only mild exercise for the first 48 hours.
  • Medicines exist to prevent altitude sickness; which, if any, is right for you is a decision for your doctor.

Our rule: Sacred Valley first

When you fly Lima–Cusco, we drive straight to the Sacred Valley, which is lower than Cusco, and stay there first. Machu Picchu (lower still) usually comes next, and Cusco city at the end, when your body has adjusted. Chinchero, on the road, is higher than Cusco, so we pass through rather than stay.

Making the first days easy

Private transfers with no waiting, gentle first-day plans, unhurried meals, and hotels chosen for comfort. If someone feels unwell, the plan changes around them — that is the point of a private trip. See Machu Picchu for seniors and private vs group tour.

See your doctor before you go

The CDC recommends pre-travel advice for high-altitude trips, especially with heart or lung conditions or regular medications. We recommend travel insurance that covers medical care and evacuation.

FAQ

How high is Cusco?

About 3,399 m (11,152 ft). The Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu are lower.

Should I go to the Sacred Valley or Cusco first?

The Sacred Valley first. It is lower, which follows the CDC principle of gradual ascent.

Does being fit prevent altitude sickness?

No. The CDC notes fitness does not change the risk; ascent speed matters most.

Can I drink alcohol on arrival?

The CDC advises avoiding alcohol for the first 48 hours at altitude.

Should I take medication for altitude?

Ask your doctor; medicines exist, and the decision depends on your health and itinerary.

Is Machu Picchu higher than Cusco?

No. Machu Picchu is about 2,430 m, roughly 1,000 m lower than Cusco.
