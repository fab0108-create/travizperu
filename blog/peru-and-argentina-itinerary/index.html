Peru and Argentina in one trip

Photo: Bernard Gagnon, CC BY-SA 4.0. Iguazú Falls, on the Argentina–Brazil border. Resized for this page.

By Francisco Arriz Barrantes · TravizPeru · Updated 24 November 2026 · Leer en español

Yes. Peru and Argentina can be one trip, with Lima and Buenos Aires as the gateways. LATAM publishes a daily direct flight from Lima to Ezeiza, and connecting flights as well. TravizPeru designs private journeys that include Iguazú or Buenos Aires, on their own or before or after Peru. Patagonia is not on that list: we design the Peru portion and coordinate the timing. Every day count below is a suggestion.

Gateways and the flight from Lima

LATAM's Lima–Buenos Aires page publishes a daily direct flight and weekly connecting flights, from Jorge Chávez (LIM) to Ministro Pistarini (EZE), in Economy and Premium Economy. The same page says frequency, aircraft and onboard service can change with the date. Confirm the flight you will actually take. Other airlines publish this city pair too. We are citing the page we read, not a complete board.

PROMPERÚ's clock table puts Buenos Aires two hours ahead of Lima. A morning landing in Buenos Aires is still a travel day. Do not put a falls visit, or a Patagonia connection, on the same clock as the Lima departure.

Buenos Aires is the city Argentina's tourism board calls the gateway into the country. Iguazú is a second flight, or a connection, not a suburb of Lima. The falls page says the national park is 17 km from the town of Puerto Iguazú, that the town has an international airport, and that buses run from town to the park. We have not treated a third-party fare site as proof of a nonstop from Lima, so check the airline for that leg. For Patagonia, the tourism board describes flights from Buenos Aires to Ushuaia, El Calafate, Bariloche and other cities, and it warns that distances inside the region are long. A Lima itinerary reaches those cities onward from Buenos Aires, unless the airline you check shows something else on your dates.

Three combinations, as suggestions

The ranges are for planning. They are not a minimum anyone official has set, and they are not a package.

  • Peru and Buenos Aires. About 8 to 12 days for Lima, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu, with the first highland nights lower than Cusco, plus about 3 to 4 nights in Buenos Aires if the city is the point of the stop. The Peru lengths on their own are in how many days in Peru.
  • Peru, Buenos Aires and Iguazú. Add the 1 to 2 days Argentina's tourism board recommends at the falls, plus the travel day into Puerto Iguazú and back. Together, without Patagonia, about 14 to 18 days. Below that, drop a place. UNESCO describes the falls as a horseshoe on the Argentina–Brazil border, about 80 m high, inscribed in 1984. The Brazilian side is a different park. This page stays with the Argentine visit the tourism board describes.
  • Peru and Patagonia. The tourism board's short trip is 7 days for El Calafate, El Chaltén and/or Ushuaia. Its longer trip is 14 to 21 days and adds places such as Bariloche and Puerto Madryn. Those figures are the board's, for Patagonia alone. Put the Peru days beside them, plus the flights through Buenos Aires. A few nights in the city can sit on the connection. They are not a free day.

If the Andes come after Argentina, start in the Sacred Valley rather than with a first night in Cusco. Cusco is 3,399 m. The CDC recommends at least 2 to 3 nights at intermediate altitude before going higher. The longer note is how we prepare for altitude. Going from Peru down to Buenos Aires does not create that problem.

Seasons that only partly overlap

PROMPERÚ's Andes are drier from April to October and rainy from November to March. Buenos Aires is temperate, with an annual mean the board gives as 18°, and the board says the city is worth visiting in any season: December to March for the summer coast, autumn colour, spring flowers, and winter from June to September.

At Iguazú the board calls the climate tropical and names autumn, March to May, as the ideal time, with 1 to 2 days. That window overlaps the start of the drier Andes season in April and May, and the tail of the Andes rains in March.

In Patagonia the board calls November to March the season for glaciers, mountains and trekking, autumn a good time for Ushuaia and El Chaltén, and June to August the season for snow sports. It also asks visitors to book lodging ahead in the high season it dates from November to April. October is still inside PROMPERÚ's dry Andes season and just before that Patagonia window. November is the start of the Andes rains and the start of the Patagonia outdoor season. June to August is dry in the Andes and winter in Patagonia. There is no month in which every region is in its easiest season. Pick the region that cannot move, and fit the others around it.

FAQ

Can Peru and Argentina be one trip?

Yes. Lima and Buenos Aires are the gateways, and LATAM publishes a daily direct flight from Lima to Ezeiza as well as connecting flights. Iguazú and Buenos Aires can be part of a private journey before or after Peru. Patagonia is not named on our Beyond Peru page: we design the Peru portion and coordinate the timing.

How do the flights work?

LATAM's Lima to Buenos Aires page publishes a daily direct flight and weekly connections, from Jorge Chávez (LIM) to Ministro Pistarini (EZE), and says frequency and aircraft can change. Puerto Iguazú has an international airport. Argentina's tourism board places the national park 17 km from the town, with buses from town to the park, and describes flights to Ushuaia, El Calafate and Bariloche as flights from Buenos Aires. Confirm the day's routing with the airline.

How many days does the combination need?

These are suggestions, not a rule. About 8 to 12 days for a focused Peru stay (Lima, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu). About 3 to 4 nights in Buenos Aires if the city is the point. Argentina's tourism board recommends 1 to 2 days at Iguazú. Its short Patagonia trip is 7 days, and its longer one is 14 to 21. Peru plus Buenos Aires plus Iguazú, without Patagonia, is about 14 to 18 days. Cutting below a range means dropping a place.

Do the seasons match?

Only in part. PROMPERÚ's drier Andes season is April to October, and the rainy season is November to March. Argentina's tourism board calls March to May the ideal time at Iguazú, November to March the outdoor season in Patagonia, and Buenos Aires pleasant all year. October sits in the dry Andes and just before that Patagonia window. November starts the Andes rains and sits inside it.

Does TravizPeru operate trips in Patagonia?

Patagonia is not named on the Beyond Peru page. TravizPeru designs the Peru portion and coordinates timing with a Patagonia segment. Iguazú Falls and Buenos Aires are the Argentina places that page already offers, on their own or before or after Peru.

What do I need to enter Argentina?

It depends on your passport. Argentina's tourism board, in its Patagonia guide, describes a passport, a return ticket and proof of funds, and says a visa is not required for most passports from Europe, the Americas and Oceania. That is not a rule for every passport. Check the official consulate for yours.
