Peru and Galápagos in one trip: how to combine them

Photo: Jorge Láscar, CC BY 2.0. Ollantaytambo, in Peru. Resized for this page.

By Francisco Arriz Barrantes · TravizPeru · Updated 24 November 2026 · Leer en español

You can combine Peru and the Galápagos in one trip, and there is no direct flight from Lima to the islands. The usual route is Lima to Quito or Guayaquil, then a second flight to Baltra or San Cristóbal. As a suggestion, allow about 14 to 18 days for a focused Peru stay, the islands, and that connection.

How you actually fly

The Galápagos National Park says the usual way in is by air, and that Quito and Guayaquil are the only cities in Ecuador with flights to the islands, because of controls on invasive species. Baltra and San Cristóbal are the two main airports. Isabela's airport is set up only for small planes on local hops between islands, so an international arrival is Baltra or San Cristóbal.

The same park page names Avianca and LATAM, and it says timetables can change at the request of the civil aviation authority or the airline. On the pages we checked, LATAM publishes a direct Quito–Baltra flight of about two hours, and a separate LATAM page publishes a direct Quito–San Cristóbal flight. Avianca publishes Guayaquil–Baltra. None of those pages lists a nonstop from Lima. The practical routing is Lima to Guayaquil or Quito, then on to the islands. Confirm the day's flights with the airline before you lock the rest of the trip.

The connection is a travel day, not a side outing. At Quito or Guayaquil, before you check in for the island flight, you still have the transit card and the biosecurity inspection described below. Build that morning into the plan. Do not schedule a Sacred Valley transfer, or a cruise boarding, on the same clock.

What you need to enter

Two payments exist, and this page does not state either amount. Read the current figures on the official pages before you travel. Older local pages still show numbers the park has replaced.

  • Transit control card. The Consejo de Gobierno del Régimen Especial de Galápagos requires a Tarjeta de Control de Tránsito for every tourist. You present a valid passport, a personal round-trip ticket between the mainland and the islands, and you pay the card fee at the Consejo counter in Quito (Mariscal Sucre) or Guayaquil (José Joaquín de Olmedo) before check-in. A pre-registration online shortens the counter. The Consejo asks you to be at the airport three hours before the flight. Keep the card until you leave. Tourist time is up to 60 days in a year, counted from the first entry, and it is not extended.
  • Biosecurity check. The park's arrival note says the Agencia de Regulación y Control de la Bioseguridad para Galápagos inspects luggage in Quito or Guayaquil before the flight, and checks the cabin bag again on arrival, so organic goods do not carry invasive species onto the islands. The same kind of check applies before you move between populated islands.
  • Park entry fee. On arrival at Baltra or San Cristóbal you pay the entry fee for the protected areas. Park rangers collect it. The categories and the current figures are on the park's page. We do not copy them here.

If you land at Baltra and you are not joining a cruise, you still cross to Santa Cruz and continue to Puerto Ayora: a bus to the Itabaca channel, a barge, then a bus or taxi on Santa Cruz. The park says a naturalist guide meets cruise passengers at the airport and takes them to the boat. San Cristóbal airport is a short ride from Puerto Baquerizo Moreno. Local transport has its own fares. Those fares are not listed here.

Peru first, or the islands first

The order is an altitude decision, then a calendar decision. PROMPERÚ puts Cusco at 3,399 m and Urubamba, in the Sacred Valley, at 2,871 m. UNESCO puts Machu Picchu at 2,430 m. The islands, and the coast you connect through, are near sea level.

The CDC Yellow Book advises against going from low altitude to a sleeping altitude above 2,750 m in one day, and recommends acclimatizing for a minimum of 2–3 nights at intermediate altitude, around 2,450–2,750 m, before going higher. Urubamba is still high. It is lower than Cusco, which is why a private Peru itinerary starts in the valley and leaves Cusco city for later. A same-day flight from the Galápagos into a first night in Cusco skips that step. Going down from the Andes to the islands does not create an altitude problem. The longer note, including what this is not (medical advice), is how we prepare for altitude.

Two workable orders:

  • Peru first when the Galápagos stay can move by a day. You finish the altitude and Machu Picchu while you still have spare days in Cusco or the valley, then fly from Lima down to sea level. We request Machu Picchu tickets as soon as you confirm, subject to availability.
  • Galápagos first when the islands are a cruise with a fixed boarding day. Put that date on the calendar first so a train delay in Peru does not cost you the boat. After the islands, start the Andes in the Sacred Valley, not with a first night in Cusco.

A land-based island stay, where you sleep in town, can usually shift a day. A cruise generally cannot. That is the practical difference, more than any rule about which country is "better" first.

How many days, as a suggestion

These ranges are suggestions for planning. They are not a minimum the authorities set, and they are not a package.

  • Peru portion, about 8 to 12 days. That is enough for Lima, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu, with nights arranged so the altitude is gradual. The Peru-only shapes, from a short trip to two weeks, are in how many days in Peru.
  • Galápagos, about 5 to 8 days. A cruise is as long as the itinerary the operator has authorized with the park. Count those nights, plus the flights on either side, and do not assume a cruise can be shortened by a day. A land-based stay can be toward the shorter end of this range if you are content with one island, and toward the longer end if you add a second.
  • The connection. Allow the travel day through Quito or Guayaquil. It is not free time inside either country.
  • Together, about 14 to 18 days. Below that, drop a place. Lake Titicaca or the Amazon on top of the islands needs more days, not faster days.

Seasons: the islands and the Andes

The islands do not follow the Andes calendar. Two public texts describe the same two island seasons with slightly different months. The Santa Cruz municipal tourism page calls January to May warm and humid, about 24–32 °C, and June to December the dry season, about 18–20 °C. A 2004 guide published by the Galápagos National Park and the Charles Darwin Foundation describes a cooler garúa season from May to November, driven by the Peru current, and a warmer season when that current weakens. Use the municipal page for a visitor's sketch of the year, and use the National Park and the Consejo de Gobierno for entry rules. Plan on a warmer, wetter half of the year and a cooler, mistier half, and treat the changeover month as variable.

PROMPERÚ describes the Peruvian Andes as drier from April to October, with sunny days and cold nights, and rainy from November to March. June, July and August sit in that drier Andes season and in the cooler Galápagos season. December to March is rainy in the Andes and warmer on the islands. Both pairings are reasonable. A wet-season visit to the citadel is its own decision: see Machu Picchu in December and January, and the countrywide calendar in the best time to visit Peru.

LATAM's Baltra page notes that July and August are busy on the islands, and that the climate is mild enough to visit in any season. Busy is not the same as closed.

Cruise or land-based

Ecuador's Special Regulation for Tourism in Protected Areas (2016) names the two ideas travelers are choosing between. A crucero navegable is a boat permitted to carry passengers overnight. It may visit only the sites on the itinerary authorized by the Galápagos National Park Directorate. You sleep on board, and the route is the route. A tour diario is a day boat: passengers may not sleep on board, the boat uses authorized visitor sites, and it returns to the port where the day started. That is the land-based pattern. You sleep in a town such as Puerto Ayora or Puerto Baquerizo Moreno and go out by day. Visitor sites in the protected areas are visited with a guide authorized for the park.

The regulation describes the concepts. The boat, the island and the dates come from the operator you choose in Ecuador. If the text has been updated since 2016, the operator and the park's current rules are the ones that apply to your dates.

FAQ

Is there a direct flight from Lima to the Galápagos?

No. The Galápagos National Park says Quito and Guayaquil are the only Ecuadorian cities with flights to the islands. LATAM publishes direct flights from Quito to Baltra and to San Cristóbal, and Avianca publishes Guayaquil to Baltra. From Lima you connect in one of those cities. Schedules change, so confirm with the airline.

How many days do Peru and the Galápagos need?

As a suggestion, not a rule, about 14 to 18 days covers a focused Peru stay (Lima, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu), the islands, and the connection. A Peru portion of about 8 to 12 days plus about 5 to 8 days in the islands is the shape we use when someone asks. Cutting below that means dropping a place, not rushing Cusco.

Should I do Peru or the Galápagos first?

Either can work if the Andes start in the Sacred Valley rather than with a first night in Cusco. Cusco is 3,399 m. A flight from the islands, which are at sea level, straight into Cusco skips the gradual ascent the CDC describes. Descending from Peru to the islands does not. If the Galápagos plan is a cruise with a fixed boarding day, put that date on the calendar first and build the Peru nights around it.

What do I need to enter the Galápagos?

A transit control card from the Consejo de Gobierno, a valid passport, a round-trip ticket to the mainland, a biosecurity luggage inspection, and the park entry fee on arrival at Baltra or San Cristóbal. The card and the fee exist; this page does not state amounts. Tourist stays are limited to 60 days in a year.

Do the seasons match?

June to August is drier in the Peruvian Andes, according to PROMPERÚ, and cooler in the Galápagos. December to March is the Andes rainy season and the warmer island season. Both combinations are workable. The changeover month in the islands is not identical in every official text.
