Peru with Grandchildren: Planning a Trip for Three Generations

Photo: Pedro Szekely, CC BY-SA 2.0. Resized for this page.

By Francisco Arriz Barrantes · TravizPeru · Updated 29 October 2026 · Leer en español

A multigenerational trip works when the slowest and the youngest set the pace. Here is how we design private Peru trips for grandparents, parents and grandchildren, using official information only.

Ages and pace

Plan one main activity per day, a rest block after lunch and short transfers. Machu Picchu Mountain, Huayna Picchu and Great Cavern are the mountain routes. The mountain routes require good physical condition and have their own requirements and restrictions; confirm the current rules on the official Ministry of Culture website (machupicchu.gob.pe) before booking. Families visit the citadel circuits together. For a slower highland pace with older travelers, see Machu Picchu for seniors.

Altitude and children

The CDC Yellow Book says children are as susceptible to altitude as adults and that slow ascent is the best approach. Young children may show only non-specific signs (poor appetite, irritability, sleep changes); older children may report headache or shortness of breath. That is why we go from the Cusco airport straight to the Sacred Valley (Urubamba 2,871 m) and leave Cusco (3,399 m) for the end (PROMPERÚ guide). Talk to your pediatrician and doctor before the trip.

Lima: an easy start at sea level

  • Magic Water Circuit (Parque de la Reserva): 13 illuminated fountains, open daily 3–10 pm, with multimedia shows at 7:15, 8:15 and 9:10 pm (official site).
  • Lima is also our base for food experiences; see Lima.

Sacred Valley days

Lower than Cusco, with space to rest. Ollantaytambo is a living Inca town and the main train station to Machu Picchu. See Sacred Valley.

Machu Picchu as a family

The train from Ollantaytambo is part of the adventure (PeruRail lists 1h30–3h30 depending on station), followed by a roughly 30-minute authorized bus up. Tickets are per person, by circuit and time slot, on tuboleto.cultura.pe. See the tickets guide, the train guide and train status. The citadel itself is on our Machu Picchu page, and the private day is the private Machu Picchu tour.

Meals

Choose places with simple options for kids, keep meals light the first days at altitude and drink bottled or treated water. Ask us about dietary needs.

Safety

Follow official advice for your nationality and our Peru safety guide. Private transfers and a guide who knows the group help keep everyone together. Get travel insurance with medical cover for every generation. How long to stay is in how many days in Peru, and the family shape of the trip is the private family tour.

FAQ

Is Peru good for multigenerational trips?

Yes, with a private, slower itinerary that starts low and adds altitude gradually.

Can young children go to Machu Picchu?

Yes, the citadel circuits are open to families. The mountain routes require good physical condition and have their own requirements and restrictions; confirm the current rules on the official Ministry of Culture website (machupicchu.gob.pe) before booking.

Do children get altitude sickness?

The CDC says children are as susceptible as adults; ascend slowly and ask your pediatrician.

Why the Sacred Valley first?

It is lower than Cusco, so the first nights are easier for all ages.

What can kids do in Lima?

The Magic Water Circuit at Parque de la Reserva is a favorite evening plan.

How do we get to Machu Picchu?

Train from Ollantaytambo (or other PeruRail stations) to Machu Picchu Pueblo, then an authorized bus.
