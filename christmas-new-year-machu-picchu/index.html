The season, honestly

December is early summer on the coast and the green, rainy season in the Andes. Mornings in the mountains can open in mist and clear by midday, or the other way round.

So we plan with the weather, not against it: a night near the citadel so you can enter early, and a second morning at Machu Picchu if the view matters to you. Rain in the Andes, January to March above all, can also delay the railway, so I build buffer around train days.

Machu Picchu tickets: what’s real this year

Since autumn 2026, entry to Machu Picchu is sold only with immediate payment on the Ministry of Culture’s platform. Nobody can hold a ticket without paying, not even us. The ticket is issued in each traveller’s name, for one route and one entry hour.

In mid-October 2026, the classic Circuit 2 was close to sold out for December. For 20–31 December we plan around the routes that still had space then: the Upper Terraces (which keep the classic postcard view), Machu Picchu Mountain and Huayna Picchu. I cannot promise Circuit 2 on those dates, and I won’t.

Before I quote, I check the official calendar for your exact date. Once you confirm, I buy your entry tickets straight away. Availability changes every day, so the earlier we look together, the more routes there are to choose from.

Cusco at Christmas and New Year

On 24 December, Cusco’s main square fills with Santurantikuy, a Christmas Eve market of handmade nativity figures, carved saints and Andean craft. On 31 December the same plaza turns into one big celebration, and many Peruvians wear yellow for luck.

Cusco sits at about 3,400 metres. If a celebration falls in your first nights, I’ll suggest sleeping in the Sacred Valley first and coming up to Cusco afterwards.
