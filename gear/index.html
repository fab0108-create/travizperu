What to pack for Machu Picchu & the Inca Trail

The mountain changes its mind many times a day. Sun, cloud, frost and rain can all come before lunch. Here is how to dress for every one of them.

Wiñay Wayna, Inca Trail · Photo: Mx. Granger, CC0

Before you fold a single shirt

Machu Picchu rests at about 2,430 metres. The road there runs through Cusco, at around 3,400 m, and the classic Inca Trail climbs to 4,215 m at Dead Woman’s Pass. On one journey you can meet cloud forest, high puna and tropical sun.

The answer is not a heavier suitcase. It is layers: pieces that go on and come off as the day changes. Below you’ll find the two seasons, the five layers that matter, and a wide choice of pieces from Columbia and The North Face, for women and men, that we think are worth packing.

Two seasons, two moods

Gold and frost, or green and rain

The dry season

May to October

Clear blue skies, sharp light and wide views from the Sun Gate. Days are warm and bright. After sunset the cold arrives quickly, and in the high camps nights can drop close to freezing.

  • A warm insulating layer for dawns and nights
  • Strong sun protection: hat, long sleeves, SPF
  • A light shell, because the cloud forest still makes its own weather

The wet season

November to April

The valleys turn emerald, orchids open, and mist moves softly through the ruins. Rain often comes in afternoon showers rather than all day. The Inca Trail closes every February for maintenance, but Machu Picchu stays open.

  • A truly waterproof shell, seam-sealed
  • Waterproof shoes with a grippy sole for wet stone
  • A pack with a rain cover, and dry bags for electronics

The short list

Before you close the suitcase

Wear & carry

  • Waterproof rain shell
  • Fleece or light insulated jacket
  • Broken-in trekking shoes
  • Hiking socks, a fresh pair for each day
  • Sun hat and sunglasses

In the daypack

  • Passport (required to enter Machu Picchu)
  • Water bottle, around 1–2 litres
  • SPF 50 sunscreen and lip balm
  • Insect repellent for the cloud forest
  • Small cash in soles

For the evenings

  • Warm hat and light gloves
  • A comfortable change of clothes
  • Headlamp for early starts
  • A dry bag for phone and camera
  • Patience for the clouds to lift. They usually do.
