Lima: Peru's Gastronomic Capital, Done Right

By Francisco Arriz Barrantes · Founder, TravizPeru · APOTUR Certified AP030-11

Most itineraries treat Lima as a one-night layover between the airport and Cusco. That's a mistake — Lima is home to some of the best restaurants in the world, a striking coastline, and neighborhoods (Miraflores, Barranco, San Isidro) worth two or three unhurried days. I design Lima stopovers and standalone city experiences that show you why chefs and food critics keep naming this city a top culinary destination.

What a private Lima experience includes

  • Reservations at award-winning restaurants — from Central and Maido to hidden local favorites tourists rarely find
  • A private guide through Barranco's art scene, colonial Lima, and the pre-Inca Huaca Pucllana ruins in the middle of the city
  • Coastal Miraflores walks along the Malecón, timed for sunset over the Pacific
  • Hand-picked boutique and five-star hotel options in Miraflores or San Isidro
  • Seamless airport transfers and connections to your next Peru destination

How I would actually use the days

One full day covers a neighborhood, Huaca Pucllana, and one serious meal. Two days let Barranco have an afternoon of its own, and the historic center another, without turning dinner into a sprint. Three days is for people who came partly for the food and are willing to let a lunch be the main event.

Central and Maido are the names most travelers already know. Tables at that level book out weeks ahead, which is why I ask for them when I know your dates, not the week you land. They are not the right table for every trip. If you would rather eat criollo food in a room that is not performing for a ranking, say so. The city supports both.

Jorge Chávez airport is in Callao, not in Miraflores. A private transfer is part of the day, so you are not negotiating a car after a long flight. I live in Lima. The arrival is the easy part of the country to get right, and it is still the part many trips waste.

How long to stay in Lima

One full day covers the highlights. Two to three days lets you properly experience the food scene and Barranco's nightlife without rushing. I typically recommend arriving a day early — before Cusco or Machu Picchu — so jet lag doesn't cost you time at altitude later in the trip.

Flying to Cusco the morning you land in Peru is how people start the Sacred Valley already tired. Cusco is about 3,400 meters. Lima is at sea level. Use that.
