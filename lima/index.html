Private Peru tours / Lima

Francisco Arriz Barrantes · APOTUR AP030-11 · Lima

Most itineraries treat Lima as a one-night layover between the airport and Cusco. That is a mistake. Lima is home to some of the best restaurants in the world, a striking coastline, and neighborhoods — Miraflores, Barranco, San Isidro — worth two or three unhurried days. I design Lima stopovers and standalone city days that show you why chefs and food critics keep naming this city a serious culinary destination.

What a private Lima experience includes

  • Reservations at restaurants worth the trip, from celebrated rooms such as Central and Maido to quieter tables tourists rarely find
  • A private guide through Barranco's art scene, colonial Lima, and the pre-Inca Huaca Pucllana ruins in the middle of the city
  • Coastal Miraflores walks along the Malecón, timed for sunset over the Pacific
  • Hand-picked boutique and five-star hotels in Miraflores or San Isidro
  • Airport transfers and a clean connection to your next stop, whether that is Machu Picchu or the Sacred Valley

How long to stay in Lima

One full day covers a clear set of highlights. Two or three days let you sit with the food and with Barranco without rushing. I typically suggest arriving a day early, before Cusco or Machu Picchu, so jet lag is spent at sea level rather than at altitude. The longer shapes are in how many days in Peru.
