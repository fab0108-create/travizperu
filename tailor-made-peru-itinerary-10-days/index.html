A 10-Day Peru Itinerary, Day by Day

Photo: Jorge Láscar from Australia / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0.

A 10-day Peru itinerary is long enough for Lima, the Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu, and Lake Titicaca, and too short for the Amazon. The order is the point: sea level first, the valley before Cusco, the lake last. What follows is that route, day by day, as a starting rhythm you can change, not a fixed departure.

What follows is one way I shape those days. It is not a departure date, and it is not a package with a code. If your children, a honeymoon, or a shorter vacation argue for less, I cut the lake before I compress the altitude. The reasoning is in how many days you need in Peru.

Why the order is the point

Lima is at sea level. Use it first, including the day you land. The Sacred Valley towns where you sleep sit lower than Cusco, which is about 3,400 meters. Machu Picchu itself is about 2,430 meters. Lake Titicaca, at 3,812 meters, comes last. Reverse that and the first memory of the trip is a headache.

Day by day

Day 1

Lima, arrival

Private transfer from Jorge Chávez. Miraflores, an early night. Nothing that has to be "achieved."

Day 2

Lima, properly

Huaca Pucllana, Barranco or the historic center, and one meal that was reserved ahead. A well-known dining room if that is the table you want and it is still open. A quieter room if it is not.

Day 3

Fly to Cusco, sleep in the valley

The flight is short. The drive down into the Sacred Valley is the point. A light afternoon. No ruin that requires a climb.

Day 4

Sacred Valley

Pisac's terraces, and either the market (Sunday, Tuesday, Thursday) or Chinchero's weavers. Maras and Moray can take the place of one of those, not all of them. The night is still in the valley.

Day 5

Ollantaytambo and the train

Morning at the living Inca town, then PeruRail or Inca Rail to Aguas Calientes. Night by the river so the citadel morning is not a race from Cusco. Which train fits the entry hour is in the Machu Picchu train guide.

Day 6

Machu Picchu

Your guide, the circuit and time we can still request, subject to availability, and the bus up from town. We do not guarantee a ticket, a circuit or an entry time. Afternoon train back toward the valley or Cusco, unless you want a second night in Aguas Calientes. This day is the private Machu Picchu tour.

Day 7

Cusco, or a slow day

The city if you have the energy. A quiet hotel day if you do not. Altitude is still real at 3,400 meters. I will not fill this day just to make it look busy.

Day 8

Cusco to Puno

The road takes about 10 hours. We stop on the altiplano when you want the day to be part of the trip, and we drive it straight when you do not. PeruRail's scenic train covers the same journey on selected days and also takes most of the day. It is not a faster option. Both routes cross a pass higher than Cusco.

Day 9

The lake

Uros, then Taquile if the night in Puno was kind. An overnight on Amantani means dropping something else. In ten days, that something is usually this comfortable lodge night, so we choose on purpose.

Day 10

Fly out of Juliaca

About 45 minutes from Puno to Juliaca, then a flight, usually through Lima. If your international connection is the same day, we protect the layover. If it is tight, the honest fix is an extra night in Lima, which is why ten days is a starting length.

What I change without being asked twice

  • A Peru honeymoon often drops the long road to Puno and keeps another night in the valley.
  • A family often shortens Day 4 and refuses a dawn train.
  • Anyone who only came for the citadel should not be talked into the lake. Five or six days, done well, beat a rushed ten.
  • The Amazon does not fit. That is the Grand Expedition, from 15 days, described with the other shapes on luxury private tours in Peru.

June through August needs an early Machu Picchu request, still subject to availability, or Day 6 moves. I would rather shift the day than hand you a circuit you will resent. The month-by-month note is best time to visit Peru.

Questions about these ten days

What is this 10-day Peru itinerary?

It runs from Lima to the Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu and Lake Titicaca, in that order. It is a starting rhythm for your dates, not a fixed group departure. The Amazon does not fit in these ten days.

Why is the order Lima, then the valley, then the lake?

Lima is at sea level, so it comes first. Sacred Valley towns sit lower than Cusco, and Machu Picchu is lower than Cusco too. Lake Titicaca is the highest stop, so it comes last.

Are Machu Picchu tickets guaranteed?

No. We request Machu Picchu tickets as soon as you confirm, subject to availability. We do not guarantee a ticket, a circuit or an entry time. The citadel day is the private Machu Picchu tour.

Can the ten days change?

Yes. A honeymoon often keeps another night in the valley and drops the long road to the lake. See the Peru honeymoon. If you only came for the citadel, a shorter trip is the better plan. Other lengths are on luxury private tours in Peru.
